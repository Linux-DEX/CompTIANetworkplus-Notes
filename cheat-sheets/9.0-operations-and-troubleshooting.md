# 9.0 Operations and Troubleshooting — cheat sheet

## Monitor and document

- Baseline a normal day before you call a spike an incident.
- Utilization, errors, discards, latency, jitter, loss, CPU, logs. A link light is not a baseline.
- QoS: classify, mark, queue, then shape (delay) or police (drop). Shape the WAN to the CIR.
- Diagrams, IP plan, circuit IDs, port map, labeled cables, config backups you have restored once. Date the diagram.

## The order

1. Identify (scope, since when, what changed). 2. Theory that fits the scope. 3. Test it. 4. Plan, including the undo. 5. One change. 6. Verify the user's task. 7. Write it down.
- One desk = cable, port, PC. A floor = switch or VLAN. A site = WAN or firewall. Do not start at the WAN for one printer.

## Tools

- Wiremap finds opens and split pairs. A certifier proves the category. Toner finds the cable. TDR = distance to a copper fault. OTDR = distance to a fiber fault. Light meter = is enough light arriving.
- iPerf separates a small link from a slow application. NetFlow says who filled the pipe. A capture says what one session did.
- SNMP polls (161) and traps (162). Use v3. Syslog is UDP 514, severity 0–7. A SIEM correlates. NTP or the times are fiction.
- HA fails at the thing you did not duplicate. Test the failover on purpose.

## Common faults

- Wireless: overlap on 2.4 GHz (use 1/6/11), sticky clients, DHCP after a good association, guest VLAN with no route.
- Copper: one dead pair links at 100 not 1000. Late collisions = duplex mismatch. Split pair fails a certifier.
- Fiber: swap TX/RX first. Clean the connector. Do not look into it.
- 169.254 = DHCP. Names fail and IPs work = DNS. Small packets work and large ones hang = MTU. Switches at 100% CPU = a loop.

## Change

- Request, approval, window, steps, backout, test, document. Emergency skips the wait, not the write-up.
- One change per window. The backout has to fit inside the window. Tell the help desk.
