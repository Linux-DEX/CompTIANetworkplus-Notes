# 6.2 Addressing Schemes and Delivery

Section **6.0 Addressing**.

## Default and custom schemes

A **default scheme** is the one a vendor ships: a home router handing out `192.168.0.0/24` or `192.168.1.0/24` from its LAN port, with itself as `.1` and as the DNS server. It works for one flat network. It collides the moment two of those routers are joined, because both sides claim the same addresses.

A **custom scheme** is the plan you write before you assign anything:

- One private block for the organization (`10.0.0.0/8` is the usual large one).
- A prefix per site, then a subnet per VLAN (users, servers, phones, guests, management).
- The same pattern at every site, so `10.site.vlan.0` means something without a spreadsheet.
- Room left to grow. A /24 for 30 people is fine. A /24 for a floor that will be 200 people is not.
- Infrastructure addresses documented: default gateway is usually the first or last usable address, and you do not mix the convention.

Public addresses, if you have them, stay on the outside of NAT (6.6). Do not invent a public range. Do not use another company's private range if you will ever build a VPN to them. Overlapping `192.168.1.0/24` on both ends of a VPN is the classic outage.

## How a packet is addressed

| Delivery | IPv4 | IPv6 | Who receives it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unicast | A normal host address | A normal host address | One interface |
| Broadcast | Host bits all 1, or `255.255.255.255` | Does not exist | Every host in the broadcast domain. Routers do not forward the limited broadcast |
| Multicast | `224.0.0.0/4` | `ff00::/8` | Hosts that joined the group |
| Anycast | The same unicast address on purpose, on more than one host | Same idea | The nearest host, by routing. Used for DNS roots and some load balancing |

**Directed broadcast** is the subnet broadcast, such as `10.1.1.255/24`. A router can be configured to forward it. Leave that off. It is how an outsider used to turn your LAN into an amplifier.

Multicast is not "broadcast but nicer" by itself. Hosts join with IGMP (5.2). Switches that do not snoop still flood it.

## Special IPv4 ranges worth grouping

| Range | Role |
|---|---|
| RFC 1918 private | `10/8`, `172.16/12`, `192.168/16`. Not routed on the Internet |
| Loopback | `127.0.0.0/8` |
| Link-local | `169.254.0.0/16`. DHCP failed |
| Documentation | `192.0.2.0/24`, `198.51.100.0/24`, `203.0.113.0/24` |
| Multicast | `224.0.0.0/4` |
| CGNAT | `100.64.0.0/10`. Carrier-grade NAT, between you and your ISP. Not yours to subnet for the office |

## Choosing a size

Count hosts, add gateway, printers, and growth, then pick the smallest prefix that fits (6.1). A VLAN for 40 phones does not get a /16. A point-to-point router link gets a /30 or /31, not a /24.

Write the scheme down in the IP plan: VLAN id, subnet, gateway, DHCP pool, and what is statically assigned. The DHCP pool must not include the static addresses, or a laptop will be handed the gateway's address.
